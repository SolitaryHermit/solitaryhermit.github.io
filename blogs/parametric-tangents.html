Tangents and Singularities of Parametric Curves

A geometric study of velocity vectors, vertical/horizontal tangents, and cuspidal points

Differential Calculus for Parametric Curves

Parametric Plane Curve & Velocity Vector

Let $C \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be a plane curve parameterized by $C(t) = \big(x(t), y(t)\big)$ where $x, y \in C^1(I)$ on an interval $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}$. The velocity vector (or tangent vector) at $t_0$ is defined as:

$$\mathbf{r}'(t_0) = \left( x'(t_0),\, y'(t_0) \right) = x'(t_0)\mathbf{i} + y'(t_0)\mathbf{j}$$
Slope of the Tangent Line

If $x'(t_0) \neq 0$, the slope $m(t_0)$ of the tangent line to the trace of $C$ at $\big(x(t_0), y(t_0)\big)$ is given by the chain rule:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \frac{y'(t_0)}{x'(t_0)}$$

The tangent line equation in point-slope form is:

$$y - y(t_0) = \frac{y'(t_0)}{x'(t_0)} \Big( x - x(t_0) \Big)$$
Classification of Tangent Behaviors
  1. Horizontal Tangent: Occurs when $y'(t_0) = 0$ and $x'(t_0) \neq 0$. Here, $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$, giving horizontal tangent line $y = y(t_0)$.
  2. Vertical Tangent: Occurs when $x'(t_0) = 0$ and $y'(t_0) \neq 0$. Here, $\left\vert{} \frac{dy}{dx} \right\vert{} \to \infty$, giving vertical line $x = x(t_0)$.
  3. Singular / Cuspidal Point ($x'(t) = 0 = y'(t)$): When $\mathbf{r}'(t_0) = (0, 0)$, the velocity vanishes. The point is singular. The tangent direction, if it exists, must be evaluated via L'Hôpital's rule or limits: $$m = \lim_{t \to t_0} \frac{y'(t)}{x'(t)}$$

  • Semicubical Parabola ($x = t^2, y = t^3$): Demonstrates a classic cusp at $t = 0$, where $x'(0) = 0$ and $y'(0) = 0$. Although the velocity vanishes, the directional limit of slopes satisfies $\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{dy}{dx} = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{3}{2}t = 0$, producing a horizontal cuspidal tangent.
  • Ellipse ($x = 2\cos t, y = 1.3\sin t$): Highlights clean alternations between vertical tangents (at $t = 0, \pm\pi$, where $x'(t) = 0$) and horizontal tangents (at $t = \pm\frac{\pi}{2}$, where $y'(t) = 0$).
  • Nodal Cubic ($x = t^2 - 1, y = t^3 - t$): Exhibits a self-intersection (node) at the origin $(0, 0)$ crossed at two distinct parameter values ($t = -1$ and $t = 1$), yielding two distinct tangent slopes at the exact same geometric point.
  • Astroid ($x = \cos^3 t, y = \sin^3 t$): Features four singular points at $t \in \{0, \frac{\pi}{2}, \pi, \frac{3\pi}{2}\}$ where $x'(t) = y'(t) = 0$. The slope limit approaches vertical or horizontal orientations as $t$ nears each cusp.

User Manual

  1. Selecting a Curve: Click any of the four preset buttons at the top of the widget to change the curve parameterization and adjust the parameter interval automatically.
  2. Inspecting Points: Drag the Parameter $t$ slider to move the evaluated point $C(t)$ along the trajectory.
  3. Playback / Animation: Click Animate to sweep continuously across the parameter interval. Click Pause to halt at any point of interest.
  4. Visual Indicators on Canvas:
    • Dark Grey Arrow: The instantaneous velocity vector $\mathbf{r}'(t) = (x'(t), y'(t))$.
    • Dashed Amber Line: Standard oblique tangent line with finite non-zero slope $dy/dx$.
    • Dashed Green Line: Horizontal tangent ($y'(t) = 0, x'(t) \neq 0$).
    • Dashed Red Line: Vertical tangent ($x'(t) = 0, y'(t) \neq 0$).
    • Dashed Red Circle: Vanishing velocity singularity ($x'(t) = 0$ and $y'(t) = 0$), indicating a point where the standard slope formula is indeterminate and requires limits.
  5. Side Inspector Panel: Displays exact analytical coordinates $C(t)$, instantaneous velocities $(x'(t), y'(t))$, speed $|\mathbf{r}'(t)|$, and computed slope $\frac{dy}{dx}$ in real time.
