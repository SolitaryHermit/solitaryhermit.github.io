Spectral Bounds in Graph Optimization

Working notes and formal statements — Updated July 2026

Foundational Definitions

(Graph Laplacian)

Let $G = (V, E)$ be an unweighted, undirected graph with $n$ vertices. The Graph Laplacian $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is defined as:

$$ \mathbf{L} = \mathbf{D} - \mathbf{A} $$

where $\mathbf{D}$ is the degree matrix and $\mathbf{A}$ is the adjacency matrix.

(Positive Semi-Definiteness)

For any real vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, the quadratic form of the Laplacian satisfies:

$$ x^\top \mathbf{L} x = \sum_{(i,j) \in E} (x_i - x_j)^2 $$

Consequently, $\mathbf{L}$ is positive semi-definite ($\mathbf{L} \succeq 0$).

Proof (Click to expand)

By definition, $x^\top \mathbf{L} x = x^\top \mathbf{D} x - x^\top \mathbf{A} x$. Expanding the sum:

$$ x^\top \mathbf{D} x = \sum_{i \in V} d_i x_i^2 \quad \text{and} \quad x^\top \mathbf{A} x = 2 \sum_{(i,j) \in E} x_i x_j $$

Combining terms yields $\sum_{(i,j) \in E} (x_i^2 - 2x_i x_j + x_j^2) = \sum_{(i,j) \in E} (x_i - x_j)^2 \ge 0$. $\blacksquare$

Spectral Properties

(Spectral Gap Bound)

Let $\lambda_2(\mathbf{L})$ denote the algebraic connectivity (second smallest eigenvalue) of $\mathbf{L}$. If $G$ is connected, then:

$$ \lambda_2(\mathbf{L}) \ge \frac{1}{n \cdot \text{diam}(G)} $$
(Mixing Time Implication)

The random walk on graph $G$ converges to the uniform distribution in $O\left(\frac{\log n}{\lambda_2}\right)$ steps.
